Native Neovim for Rust: Docs Under the Cursor

November 2026

When you write iterator chains, you keep needing the same thing: what does this method take, and what does it give back? Neovim can tell you without a plugin or a browser tab. This post collects those habits one at a time, starting with the smallest one.

Everything here is built into Neovim. It assumes rust-analyzer is running as the language server, which is the part that knows about Rust.

1. K: docs for the thing under the cursor

Put the cursor on a method name, say find, in normal mode, and press K.

self.tasks.iter_mut().find(|t| t.id == id)
//                     ^ cursor here, press K

A floating window opens with the signature and the docs:

fn find<P>(&mut self, predicate: P) -> Option<Self::Item>
where
    P: FnMut(&Self::Item) -> bool,

That's often all you need. Here, it tells you that the closure gets a reference to each item, and that find hands back an Option, not another iterator.

Press K a second time to jump into the window. You can then scroll and yank in it like any buffer, and q closes it.

Since Neovim 0.10, K calls vim.lsp.buf.hover() whenever a language server is attached to the buffer. You don't need to map it yourself. Before that, K ran man on the word under the cursor, and it still does in buffers with no language server. See :help K and :help lsp-defaults.
